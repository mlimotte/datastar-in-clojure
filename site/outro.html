And here's where it all comes together. The capstone is the whole of Brew Board in clojure/src/brewboard: the hand-written SSE writer, the menu and pure handle-command, whole-view renders for the customer and the board, the broadcast hub, the reitit routes, and the native-shell routes. Every piece you built, in one place. Start it and open the two pages side by side (two browsers, or one normal and one private window, so they get different sid cookies, because part 6 taught you what happens otherwise):

cd clojure && clojure -M -m brewboard.main      # PORT env var, default 8080
# customer: http://localhost:8080/      board: http://localhost:8080/board

Add a medium oat latte as Ada, place the order, advance it twice on the board, and mark oat milk sold out. Then just watch: every open page follows along, and the customer's drop-down shows oat as sold out without a reload. It's a small thing, and it's oddly thrilling the first time. clojure -M -m brewboard.demo runs the same session headless over real HTTP and prints what each stream received, for those of us who like to see the receipts.

One more thing. native/ios and native/android hold small reference shells, a WKWebView app and a WebView app, that load the same server, apply the served /native/path-config rules, and implement the BrewShell bridge from part 7. They aren't built in CI (Xcode in CI is its own adventure); native/README.md says what was checked and how to run them against localhost.
